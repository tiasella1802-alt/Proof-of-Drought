      From rain to phone, step by step

      1. Three records watch the rain

        Copernicus ERA5 (Europe) and NASA MERRA-2 are weather models fed with satellite, weather-station and balloon measurements. CHIRPS is satellite rainfall checked against rain gauges.

      2. Three oracles post the readings

        Each posts its record's reading, an AI check on the data and a seal of the raw numbers. In this prototype we run all three. In a real scheme each would be a different organisation, for example a weather service, a university and a reinsurer.

      3. The contract decides and pays

        Two of the three records must agree. Then it pays everyone in the district at once. Here it uses test money; a real scheme would hold a digital Fiji dollar.

      4. The money reaches the phone

        A licensed payout partner would turn it into M-PAiSA or MyCash mobile money and send an SMS. Not built yet: it needs a partner and approval from the Reserve Bank of Fiji.

      Simulated

      The 96 farmers, their wallets and the money are test versions · district points are approximate · the payout rules and pricing margin are this project's proposal; a real scheme would set them with an insurer and the regulator

      Built for COP31

      COP31 meets in Antalya in November 2026. Its Pre-COP ministers met in October in Nadi, Fiji, one of the eight districts this cover protects. It builds on parametric insurance already working in the Pacific (PCRIC, UNDP's PICAP).

      What we haven't solved yet

      • Payouts can miss the harvest. Fiji only publishes cane yields for the whole country, so we can't check district payouts against district harvests. In 1989–90 and 2004–05 the cover paid in a dry season that the cane survived.
      • El Niño years are expensive. The fair price can be more than three times the normal one. Sponsors could cover the difference, but no sponsor has signed up.
      • Nobody in Fiji has reviewed this yet. Not farmers, the Sugar Cane Growers Fund, the Fiji Meteorological Service or an insurer. Their input would change the rules.
      • We run every oracle, with test money. The contract runs on a public test network. Real oracles need independent operators.
      • A disagreement slows the payout down. If Copernicus and NASA disagree, the decision waits for CHIRPS, which comes out about three weeks after each month. That happened at about 1 in 9 past checkpoints. CHIRPS's quicker preliminary version could cut that to days.
      • The AI is deliberately small. A check for faked data and a one-input forecast that is only a little better than the long-run average.
