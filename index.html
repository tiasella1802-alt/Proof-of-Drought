This drought cover would have paid them in January 1998: automatically, the moment two independent satellite records agreed the rain had failed.

      This builds on what already works

      Parametric insurance, which pays on a measured trigger rather than an assessed claim, already protects Pacific countries and, more recently, households. The Pacific Catastrophe Risk Insurance Company insures governments; UNDP's Pacific Insurance and Climate Adaptation Programme has paid out in Fiji in 2023 and 2025; a microinsurance scheme paid 15,295 people in Papua New Guinea after the 2026 drought. What this project adds:

      • Rules anyone can read and no one can bend. The trigger is public code on a blockchain, not a document at an insurer.
      • Two independent data sources must agree, so no single provider, glitch or insider can trigger or block a payout.
      • An early payout in the worst droughts, in January, while there's still time to save stock and replant.
      • Every reading is verifiable against the public archives by anyone, including the farmer.
      • The full backtest is published, false alarms included.

      Climate data you can trust

      The ClimateChain track asks for reliable, verifiable climate data. Here that data moves money, so it has to be provably right.

      Scales on free global data

      ERA5 and NASA POWER cover every farm on Earth. A new district needs coordinates and a crop calendar, not a weather station.

      What's real, and what isn't

      Real

      • Daily rainfall and soil moisture, 1981 to today, for all 8 districts: Copernicus ERA5 / ERA5-Land (via Open-Meteo) and NASA POWER (MERRA-2)
      • Fiji sugarcane yields 1961–2024: FAOSTAT
      • The 1998 timeline and figures: UN OCHA situation report, 16 December 1998
      • The smart contract and every transaction you see, executed by a real Ethereum Virtual Machine

      Simulated

      • The 96 farmers, their wallets and the money: test funds on a private chain in your browser
      • District points are approximate centres of each cane sector, not farm boundaries
      • Payout rules were set by this project; a real scheme would set them with an insurer and the regulator
      • Yields are national, so district-level payouts can't be checked against district harvests
